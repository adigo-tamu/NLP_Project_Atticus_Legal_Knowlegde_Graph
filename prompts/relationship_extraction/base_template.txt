RELATIONSHIP TYPES TO EXTRACT:

1. **REFERENCES**: Entity A references or mentions Entity B
   - Examples: "as defined in Section 3", "pursuant to the License Agreement"
   - Directionality: Source entity → Referenced entity

2. **OBLIGATES**: Party A obligates Party B to perform an action
   - Examples: "Seller shall deliver", "Licensee must pay"
   - Directionality: Obligating party → Obligated party → Obligation
   - Properties: deadline, conditions

3. **GRANTS_RIGHT**: Party A grants a right to Party B
   - Examples: "Licensor grants Licensee the right to", "may terminate"
   - Directionality: Granting party → Receiving party → Right
   - Properties: conditions, exclusivity

4. **GOVERNS**: Jurisdiction or law governs a contract/clause
   - Examples: "governed by Delaware law", "subject to jurisdiction of"
   - Directionality: Jurisdiction → Document/Clause
   - Properties: scope (governing law, venue)

5. **DEFINES**: Clause or section defines a legal concept
   - Examples: "Software means...", "Confidential Information includes..."
   - Directionality: Defining clause → Legal concept
   - Properties: definition text

6. **MODIFIES**: Clause modifies another clause
   - Examples: "notwithstanding Section 3", "except as provided in"
   - Directionality: Modifying clause → Modified clause
   - Properties: modification_type (exception, amendment, clarification)

7. **DEPENDS_ON**: Clause/obligation depends on another clause/obligation
   - Examples: "if Party A breaches Section 2, then...", "subject to Section 5"
   - Directionality: Dependent item → Prerequisite item
   - Properties: dependency_type (conditional, prerequisite)

8. **CONTRADICTS**: Potential conflict between clauses
   - Examples: Conflicting obligations, inconsistent terms
   - Directionality: Clause 1 ↔ Clause 2 (bidirectional)
   - Properties: conflict_type, severity

9. **TEMPORALLY_PRECEDES**: Event/obligation must occur before another
   - Examples: "payment due 30 days after delivery", "prior to termination"
   - Directionality: Earlier event → Later event
   - Properties: time_gap, temporal_constraint

10. **FINANCIALLY_RELATES**: Financial relationship between entities
    - Examples: "Licensee shall pay $50,000 to Licensor"
    - Directionality: Payer → Payment → Payee
    - Properties: amount, frequency, purpose

EXTRACTION GUIDELINES - CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT REASONING:

For each relationship, follow these steps:

**Step 1: Identify Candidate Entity Pairs**
- Look for entities that appear in the same sentence or nearby sentences
- Consider semantic proximity, not just textual distance
- Focus on entity pairs that could logically have a relationship

**Step 2: Analyze Context**
- Read the surrounding text carefully
- Identify verbs, prepositions, and connectors between entities
- Look for legal language patterns (shall, must, may, pursuant to, subject to)

**Step 3: Determine Relationship Type**
- Match the context to one of the 10 relationship types
- Consider the directionality (who/what affects whom/what)
- Identify the most specific relationship type

**Step 4: Extract Evidence**
- Quote the exact text that supports the relationship
- Include the complete sentence or clause
- Ensure the evidence clearly demonstrates the relationship

**Step 5: Assign Confidence**
- 1.0: Explicit relationship with clear legal language
- 0.9: Strong contextual evidence
- 0.7-0.8: Good inference from context
- 0.5-0.6: Ambiguous or uncertain relationship

**Step 6: Extract Properties**
- Identify relationship-specific properties
- Extract temporal information, conditions, amounts, etc.
